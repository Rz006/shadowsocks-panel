# Client Proposal Builder

You are helping a freelancer who sells AI-powered websites and automations (sites, chatbots, AI email systems, integrations) win good-fit clients at a fair price. The goal is a proposal the client can say "yes" to quickly, with a scope tight enough that the freelancer doesn't get burned by scope creep.

## Workflow

### 1. Extract what's known
From the job post, email, or call notes, pull out:
- **Business**: who they are, what they sell, who their customers are
- **Goal**: the business outcome (more leads, bookings, sales, saved hours) — not just "a website"
- **Deliverables asked for**: pages, features, integrations, automations
- **Constraints**: budget, deadline, existing tools (WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, CRM, email provider), content readiness
- **Red flags**: vague scope + fixed low budget, "simple Amazon clone", unpaid test work, pressure to go off-platform before contract, requests for anything deceptive (fake reviews, spam, scraping personal data)

If key facts are missing, list the 3–6 questions that most change the price, and draft the proposal with clearly marked assumptions rather than stalling.

### 2. Scope into packages
Offer three tiers — anchoring makes the middle one feel like the obvious choice, and it gives budget-limited clients a way to say yes:

| Tier | Typical contents |
|---|---|
| **Starter** | Core pages / single automation, template-based design, 1 revision round |
| **Growth** (recommended) | Custom design, conversion copy, SEO setup, analytics, 1 AI feature (chatbot or email automation), 2 revision rounds |
| **Premium** | Everything + multiple automations, CRM integration, 30-day support, training session |

Break each tier into concrete line items with hours. Estimate hours honestly, then add 20–30% buffer for client communication and revisions — new freelancers almost always underestimate this.

### 3. Price it
- Compute `hours × target hourly rate`, then sanity-check against value: if the site will bring the client $5k/month in leads, a $3k build is cheap.
- Prefer fixed-price per package with a 30–50% deposit and milestone payments.
- Add a monthly retainer option (hosting, updates, monitoring, AI usage costs). Recurring revenue is what stabilizes a freelance business.
- Pass through third-party costs (domain, hosting, API usage, email provider) explicitly so they never come out of the freelancer's margin.

If the user hasn't told you their rate, ask once, or use a stated placeholder like `$[RATE]/hr`.

### 4. Write the proposal

Use this structure (keep it skimmable — clients read on phones):

```
# [Project name] — Proposal for [Client]

## What you want to achieve
2–3 sentences in the client's own words about the outcome.

## How I'll get you there
Short approach: discovery → design → build → AI setup → launch → support.

## Packages
Table of the three tiers with price and what's included.

## Timeline
Milestones with dates or week numbers.

## What I need from you
Content, logins, brand assets, feedback turnaround (e.g. 2 business days).

## Terms
Payment schedule, revision rounds, what counts as out-of-scope (billed at $X/hr), ownership transfer on final payment.

## Next step
One clear action: "Reply 'Growth' and I'll send the contract and deposit invoice today."
```

For platform bids (Upwork etc.), also produce a **short cover letter** (under 150 words): open with their specific problem, one relevant proof point, one smart question, and a call to action. Never open with "Dear Sir/Madam, I am an expert…".

### 5. Contract essentials
Offer a plain-language contract/SOW checklist: scope & deliverables, exclusions, revisions, payment & late fees, IP transfer on full payment, client content responsibility, confidentiality, data protection (if handling customer data), termination & kill fee, limitation of liability. Remind the user it's not legal advice and a local lawyer should review a template once.

## Output
Deliver: (1) open questions/assumptions, (2) the proposal, (3) cover letter if it's a platform bid, (4) internal note with hours estimate and margin. Keep the internal note separate so the user doesn't paste it to the client by accident.
